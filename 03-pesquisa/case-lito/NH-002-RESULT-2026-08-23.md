# CASE-LITO — NH-002 RESULT

Date: 2026-08-23
Mission: Natural History + Phenotype Breaker

## Scope
Literature-level comparison only. No patient-specific phenotype assignment. Public reports are not a clinical record.

## Primary findings
1. The MRC Prion Disease Rating Scale is a validated 20-point functional measure spanning cognition, speech, mobility, personal care/feeding and continence. Natural-history cohorts show heterogeneous trajectories, including fast linear decline, slow linear decline and low-score preterminal plateaus.
2. sCJD is phenotypically heterogeneous. Published frameworks include visual, ataxic, cognitive, psychiatric/behavioural, sleep/thalamic, stroke-like and classical presentations.
3. Heidenhain is specifically an early/initial visual presentation; visual symptoms with temporarily preserved cognition are therefore not by themselves evidence against sCJD.
4. The 2026 MM/MV2C cortical-subtype cohort is a useful breaker: it is predominantly early-cognitive and relatively slow (mean duration ~16 months), with motor signs generally later. On the currently public chronology, this does not provide a clean match for a motor-prominent early trajectory.
5. Other molecular subtypes can show ataxic/motor-first or delayed-cognitive patterns, but subtype assignment requires clinical/laboratory data unavailable publicly and ultimately clinicopathologic/molecular evidence.

## Breaker result
No literature phenotype tested here uniquely explains the public trajectory strongly enough to support patient-specific subtype assignment.

VERDICT: NO_DIFFERENTIATION
Confidence: moderate for the negative claim; low for any positive phenotype claim.

## Critical unknowns
- true neurologic symptom onset and ordering
- serial MRC-PDRS or equivalent functional measurements
- MRI DWI/FLAIR chronology
- CSF RT-QuIC and other biomarkers
- PRNP genotype/codon 129 if performed
- EEG chronology

## Operational consequence
Do not spend access-window time trying to label a subtype from public reports. For trial access, prioritize formal screening by study investigators and functional eligibility. Preserve phenotype work as scientific context, not a gatekeeping diagnosis.
